The Licence Is The One To Fix Today
California Business & Professions Code §7030.5

California requires a licensed contractor to display their licence number in all forms of advertising, and the Contractors State Licence Board treats a website as advertising. We could not find licence 1119278 anywhere on nicksroofingservices.com.

Two reasons this matters beyond compliance. Californians are told repeatedly to check a contractor's licence before hiring, and a roofer whose number is not on their site looks like a roofer who does not have one. And you have a genuinely good licence position — active, bonded at $25,000 — and you are getting no credit for it.

The estimate you cannot actually request

"Free Instant Estimate" appears as a call to action. There is no form behind it — the link goes to a contact page, and the homepage contains no form fields at all. The word "instant" is doing the opposite of its job. Someone in a hurry clicks, finds no way to submit anything quickly, and goes back to the search results.

And the number is not tappable — there are three of them
510-815-4451 your website — plain text, no tel: link (510) 453-3048 BuildZoom listing (510) 944-2118 contractor listings

A homeowner on a phone has to memorise your number and dial it manually. And three numbers in public circulation splits your call tracking and confuses anyone who finds two of them. Pick one and correct the others, or calls keep landing somewhere you are not listening.

Estimated Size — And An Honest Word About The Target
From public permit and licence records

Revenue roughly $200,000 to $450,000. As a sole ownership with a small crew, most of the margin is owner earnings rather than net profit in the corporate sense.

Public permit records show 14 permitted projects over two years — 5 in 2024 and 9 in 2025, at typical project values of $5,000 to $20,000 and an average around $8,000. That is roughly $72,000 of permitted work last year. Repairs, coatings and smaller jobs generally do not require permits and typically run two to four times permitted volume for a roofer your size. The trend is up: nine permitted projects against five is close to a doubling.

The part worth being straight about

You said you need five to ten more roofing jobs a month. That is sixty to a hundred and twenty additional jobs a year. Against nine permitted re-roofs last year, that is not an increase in leads — it is a different size of business: more crews, more trucks, more scheduling, a different cash-flow shape. Achievable, and the market is certainly big enough, but it is a two to three year build rather than something a website or an ad account delivers on its own.

A realistic first step from where you are: doubling again, to roughly eighteen to twenty-four permitted jobs, which is what the fixes in this document could support without hiring. Worth having that conversation before anyone quotes you for lead generation.
